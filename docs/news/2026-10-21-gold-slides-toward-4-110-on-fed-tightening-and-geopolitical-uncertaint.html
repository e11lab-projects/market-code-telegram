Gold prices declined on Wednesday, retreating toward the $4,110 level. The pullback occurred as conflicting signals surrounding diplomatic efforts to resolve the U.S.-Iran war created fresh uncertainty across financial markets. At the same time, lingering expectations of additional monetary tightening by the Federal Reserve continued to exert downside pressure on the precious metal.

The geopolitical friction directly influences the transmission channel for bullion through both inflation expectations and monetary policy path adjustments. Prolonged military conflicts tend to raise fears of persistent inflationary pressures, which in turn fuels expectations that the Fed will maintain higher interest rates or implement further policy tightening. Because gold does not generate yield, elevated interest rate expectations increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion relative to interest-bearing assets.

On one hand, any definitive breakdown in peace initiatives or further escalation in conflict could reinforce gold's role as a traditional safe-haven hedge against geopolitical risk. On the other hand, if geopolitical tensions sustain inflationary pressures and push the Federal Reserve toward an even more aggressive tightening trajectory, higher expected rates could deepen selling pressure across the gold market.
