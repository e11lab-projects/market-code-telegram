Weak US Housing Data Clashes With Fed Rate Hike Considerationsទិន្នន័យគេហដ្ឋានអាមេរិកចុះខ្សោយស្របពេល Fed ពិចារណាដំឡើងអត្រាការប្រាក់

Fresh US housing reports showed renewed deterioration in September as policymakers evaluate interest rate moves. The data could influence gold by shifting expectations around Federal Reserve tightening and economic momentum.

Economic reports released on Tuesday showed that US housing indicators deteriorated further during September, highlighting growing stress in interest-rate-sensitive segments of the economy. The fresh weakness emerged at a sensitive juncture, as Federal Reserve officials continue to weigh whether further interest rate hikes are necessary to keep inflation in check.

The primary transmission mechanism to XAU/USD runs through monetary policy expectations, the US dollar, and Treasury yields. The housing sector often serves as an early indicator of monetary tightening transmitting into real economic activity. When real estate metrics slump, investors frequently assess whether tighter monetary policy is nearing its practical limits, which can soften the greenback and ease upside pressure on sovereign bond yields.

Because gold is a non-yielding asset quoted in US dollars, its trajectory is closely tethered to shifts in monetary tightening. Downside surprises in housing activity traditionally support gold if traders anticipate that cooling economic activity will discourage the central bank from delivering additional rate increases. A pullback in the US dollar typically makes bullion more attractive for overseas buyers.

Conversely, if Fed policymakers view persistent inflation risks as outweighing housing softness, expectations of sustained higher interest rates can remain intact. In that scenario, elevated borrowing costs and resilient nominal yields would typically generate headwinds for gold by raising the opportunity cost of holding the metal.

Traders will be watching upcoming macroeconomic reports and Federal Reserve communication to determine whether broader economic indicators mirror the housing slowdown, or if policymakers maintain a hawkish stance despite sector-specific cooling.
