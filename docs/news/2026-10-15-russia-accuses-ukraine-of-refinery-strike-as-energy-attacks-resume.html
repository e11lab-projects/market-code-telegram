Ukraine ended its pause on striking energy targets after new Russian attacks, prompting Russian accusations of a refinery strike. Heightened geopolitical tension typically spurs safe-haven buying in gold.

Geopolitical friction escalated after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky declared that a pause on striking energy infrastructure has concluded. The announcement followed fresh Russian attacks, prompting Ukraine to resume targeting energy facilities. In the wake of this policy shift, Russian authorities accused Ukraine of carrying out an attack against an oil refinery.

For the gold market, military developments that directly target energy installations serve as a critical transmission channel. Heightened geopolitical conflict frequently triggers safe-haven buying as investors seek protection from market unpredictability. Furthermore, disruptions to refining capacity and fuel supplies carry the potential to influence energy prices, which can revive broader inflation concerns and drive hedging activity into precious metals like XAU/USD.

While safe-haven demand typically provides immediate support for gold, rising energy risks can also stoke fears of persistent inflation. If financial markets interpret potential energy supply disruptions as a reason for central banks to maintain elevated interest rates, higher bond yields and a firmer US dollar could dampen gold's momentum.

Conversely, if the situation stabilizes quickly without significant fallout in energy markets or broader escalation, safe-haven risk premiums could rapidly evaporate, leaving bullion vulnerable to underlying interest rate pressures.
